## F-C2 — common-phase control

Applied in §4.2 after the original surrogate result. The paragraph states:

- the original surrogate randomizes the two correction components independently and destroys their cross-spectrum;
- a control applying the same random phase to both components preserves the full component cross-spectrum exactly (relative error `~2e-16`) while randomizing phase relative to the drift;
- it still leaves RMS divergence `3.66e-2` versus `2.75e-2` for the uncorrected drift (`1.33x`), compared with `2.2e-15` for the exact projection;
- the tested surrogate represents per-mode variance matching (the operational standard); a control preserving the full component cross-spectrum also fails; the failure is therefore structural, not an artifact of how aggressively phases were scrambled.

Use “component cross-spectrum” precisely. Global/k-dependent unitary rotations preserve vector energy but not the component cross-spectrum; do not present them as cross-spectrum-preserving controls. The paper's single-realization `3.76e-2` is reproduced by the dynamic run (`3.7565e-2`).
